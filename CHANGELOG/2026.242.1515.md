# 2026.242.1515 — feat(cstream): gate the frame that reaches a CONSUMER, not the one produced

## Summary

Adds `cstream-frame-through-webrtc` — **the only check in this bed that can fail on an encoding or
transport defect** — and advances the two pins that make the method resolvable.

Everything else in this bed that looks at pixels looks at the **wrong end** of the pipeline:

| check | reads | fails on an encode/transport defect? |
|---|---|---|
| `cstream-stream-live` | the producer's own pipeline tap | **no** — passes while nothing leaves the process |
| the 10 `wl-*` checks | the compositor | **no** — never touch the encoder or the wire |
| `cstream-frame-through-webrtc` | what a **real consumer decodes** | **yes** |

**That gap is not theoretical.** It is exactly what let a missing `webrtcbin` hide: this bed was
green at 21 checks against a stack that negotiated WebRTC sessions and delivered **not one byte** to
a consumer, because `webrtcsink` failed to build its session pipeline with a WARN and every
producer-side check passed regardless (`opencharly/pod-cstream#7`).

```yaml
- check: a frame pulled back THROUGH the negotiated WebRTC track has real content
  id: cstream-frame-through-webrtc
  context: [runtime]
  cstream:
      method: frame
      artifact: /tmp/cstream-frame.jpg
      artifact_min_bytes: 5000
      artifact_not_uniform: true
```

`artifact_not_uniform` is the assertion that matters. A stream that negotiates and then sends black,
or a placeholder, is the failure this check exists to catch — and a byte count alone would pass it.

**Pins advanced** to the published tags that make the method resolvable:

```
layer-cstream-desktop  v2026.242.1424 -> v2026.242.1505   ships /usr/local/bin/cstream-frame-probe
plugin-cstream         v2026.242.1148 -> v2026.242.1502   serves `cstream: frame`
```

The bed goes from 21 checks to **22**.
